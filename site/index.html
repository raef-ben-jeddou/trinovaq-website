The problem

Security is still a second project.

Most teams build the product first and assemble the security case afterwards. By then the expensive decisions have already been made.

  1. Findings arrive late

    Weaknesses surface in system testing or after release, when every fix costs the most.

  2. Evidence is manual

    Threat models, test results and requirements sit in separate spreadsheets, linked by hand.

  3. Audit week is a scramble

    The trail from risk to proof gets stitched together under deadline pressure.

Platform

From threat model to audit trail.

Four capabilities that follow the same thread: what could go wrong, where the product is weak, whether the defences hold, and how you prove it.

Threat analysis and risk assessment

Map what is worth protecting, how it could be attacked and what that would cost. Every threat scenario gets a feasibility rating, an impact rating and a risk decision you can stand behind.

  • Assets and damage scenarios
  • Attack paths
  • Risk treatment decisions

Vulnerability detection

Catch the weaknesses in your software before someone else does. Findings are ranked by severity, so the team fixes what matters first and can show what was fixed.

  • Severity ranking
  • Fix tracking
  • Findings linked to threats

Embedded security testing

We test the real device, not only the design. Interfaces, firmware and communication are attacked the way an adversary would, and each result is recorded against the threat it answers.

  • Hardware interfaces
  • Firmware
  • Communication

Compliance and evidence

Every requirement mapped to the work that satisfies it. Reports your assessor can read are produced while the team works, not assembled the week before the audit.

  • Requirement mapping
  • Gap view
  • Audit-ready reports

How it works

One finding, followed all the way through.

  1. Model

    Describe the product: its parts, its interfaces and what it has to protect.

    Asset · Control commands

  2. Analyse

    Identify the threats, rate the risk and find the weaknesses that make an attack possible.

    TS-014 · Spoofed command · risk 5

  3. Test

    Attack the real device and confirm that every control holds.

    TC-033 · Replay rejected · pass

  4. Prove

    Give your assessor a trail that links each requirement to its evidence.

    EV-207 · Linked to requirement

Automotive

Cybersecurity for the automotive industry.

Vehicles are among the most tightly regulated connected products. We support OEMs and Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers working to UN R155 and ISO/SAE 21434.

UN R155

The UN regulation that makes a cybersecurity management system a condition of vehicle type approval.

ISO/SAE 21434

The engineering standard for cybersecurity across the whole vehicle lifecycle, from concept to decommissioning.
